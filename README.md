## The short answer

In passages about sin and eternal life, **salvation is God’s rescue through Jesus Christ from sin, wrath, and condemnation, bringing people from death to life**. Matthew 1:21 says Jesus will save his people from their sins. Romans 5:8–11 connects that rescue to his death and deliverance from wrath. John 5:24 connects it to passing from death to life.

**When does that happen?** John 5:24 supplies the clearest present-tense answer in this study. The person who hears Jesus’ word and believes the one who sent him **has everlasting life** and **has passed from death to life**. Acts 16:30–34 gives a direct answer to what a person must do to be saved: believe on the Lord Jesus Christ. The passage continues with hearing the word and baptism. First Peter 1:3–9 also speaks of salvation yet to be revealed, alongside an inheritance and believers being kept through faith. The KJV describes both present possession and a future outcome. Taken together, these passages leave some questions about the exact sequence of faith, repentance, baptism, and final salvation open to interpretation.

## Key findings

| Finding | Passages to read together |
| --- | --- |
| **God grants salvation by grace through faith in Christ.** Ephesians pairs the gift of salvation with a life of good works. Titus pairs God’s mercy with regeneration, renewal, and the hope of eternal life. | Ephesians 2:4–10; Titus 3:4–7; Acts 4:10–12 |
| **The response includes belief, repentance, confession, and baptism.** Romans pairs confessing Jesus as Lord with believing that God raised him from the dead. Peter calls his hearers to repent and be baptized, and links this response with forgiveness and the gift of the Holy Ghost. First Peter connects baptism to a good conscience toward God and Jesus’ resurrection. These passages should be read together when asking how baptism relates to receiving salvation. | Romans 10:8–13; Acts 2:36–41; Acts 16:25–34; 1 Peter 3:18–22 |
| **The KJV tests professed faith by what it does.** James asks whether a claim to faith without works can save and calls such faith dead. Jesus distinguishes saying “Lord, Lord” from doing the Father’s will, and Matthew 25 connects inheriting the kingdom with acts of mercy. Ephesians says salvation is God’s gift and gives good works as the purpose of the life he creates. | James 2:14–26; Matthew 7:21–23; Matthew 25:31–46; Ephesians 2:8–10 |
| **The warnings about enduring are part of the picture.** In Matthew and Mark, Jesus speaks of enduring to the end in the context of persecution and distress. First Peter speaks of faith tested now and salvation to be revealed. | Matthew 24:9–14; Mark 13:9–13; 1 Peter 1:3–9 |
| **Read the whole rich-ruler exchange.** Jesus tells the man who asks about entering life to keep the commandments, then tells him to give to the poor and follow him. When the disciples ask who can be saved, Jesus points to what is possible with God. Each reply belongs to the same conversation. | Matthew 19:16–26 |

The word *save* also appears in accounts of rescue from drowning, enemies, and other immediate danger. It can even mean *except*. Those uses help explain the search results without treating every match as a statement about eternal life. Compare Matthew 14:28–31 with Matthew 1:21 and John 5:24.
